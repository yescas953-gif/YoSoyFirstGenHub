🚦 Resume Do's & Don'ts

Instant Checklist
✅
GREEN FLAGS — Do These
✓
Start bullets with action verbs
"Led 20+ students in weekly tutoring sessions" — not "Was responsible for tutoring"
✓
Use numbers to show impact
"Volunteered 150+ hours" · "Raised $400" · "Served 200 patients"
✓
Keep it to exactly 1 page
Set margins to 0.75" and font to 11pt if you're running over
✓
List your GPA if it's 3.0 or above
Include it under Education. Also list AP, IB, or Honors courses you've taken
✓
Tailor it to each program
Match the program's keywords. USC YRP wants "research interest." Riordan wants "leadership."
✓
Save and send as a PDF
File → Download → PDF in Google Docs. Never submit a .doc or .pages file
✓
Include languages you speak
Bilingual in Spanish? Write "Spanish — Native/Fluent." In LA, this is a huge advantage
✓
Get it reviewed before submitting
Ask your school counselor, a teacher, or a trusted adult to read it first
🚫
RED FLAGS — Avoid These
✗
Including a photo or personal info
Never include your photo, date of birth, full address, or social security number
✗
Using an unprofessional email
"xXgamer99Xx@gmail.com" gets ignored. Create "firstname.lastname@gmail.com" for applications
✗
Writing vague, passive descriptions
"Helped with things" or "Was a member of" tells them nothing. Be specific and active
✗
Going over 1 page
You are in high school. No exceptions. Cut the least important thing before adding more
✗
Lying or exaggerating
Interviewers ask follow-up questions. If you said "led a team of 50" you need to back it up
✗
Spelling and grammar errors
Use Grammarly (free) or read it backward word-by-word to catch mistakes. One typo can cost you
✗
Using fancy fonts or colors
Stick to Calibri, Times New Roman, or Garamond in black. Colorful resumes look unprofessional
✗
Sending the same resume everywhere
A resume for USC YRP should mention research. A Riordan resume should mention leadership. Customize!

🌱 FINISHING 8TH GRADE → ENTERING 9TH

Get a Head Start This Summer

The summer before 9th grade is your first real opportunity. Most students do nothing. You can be different — and it will show on your applications four years from now.

💡 Why 8th Grade Matters for College

📐 Math Placement
If you take Algebra in 8th grade, you can reach Calculus by 12th — which top colleges love to see.
📖 Reading Habits
Students who read over the summer score significantly higher on the SAT verbal section. Start now.
🏃 Find Your Thing Early
Colleges want to see 4 years of commitment. Starting in 9th grade is already late. Starting this summer is smart.
🧾 Volunteer Hours
Volunteer hours from this summer count. Start logging now.

📘 9th Grade — Build the Foundation

You have 4 years. Every step you take now makes senior year easier.

📚 Academics
Take the most challenging classes you can handle. Colleges see your full 9–12 transcript. Aim for at least 1 honors or AP course if offered.
🏃 Activities
Join 1–2 clubs, sports, or programs you genuinely enjoy. Quality over quantity — stick with things long enough to grow in them.
❤️ Volunteer
Start logging volunteer hours now. Even 1–2 hours/month adds up. Use programs on this site — LAPL, YMCA, or animal shelters.
🤝 Relationships
Build a real relationship with at least one teacher or counselor. They'll write your recommendation letters in 11th grade — start now.
💭 Start Thinking
What do you like? What problems do you want to solve? Start a journal of experiences and ideas — your future essays will come from this.
💰 FAFSA Awareness
Ask your parents to create a FSA ID now at studentaid.gov — this is required for FAFSA and takes time. Don't wait until 12th grade.

📙 11th Grade — The Most Important Year

Junior year is the single most important year for college admissions. Take it seriously.

📝 SAT / ACT
Take the SAT or ACT in fall AND spring. Aim to improve each time. Khan Academy offers FREE official SAT prep — use it every day for 30 min.
🏅 PSAT/NMSQT
Take the PSAT/NMSQT in October — this is your shot at National Merit Scholarship. Score in the top 1% and you qualify for life-changing awards.
✉️ Rec Letters
Ask 2 teachers for rec letters in April or May — BEFORE summer. Give them a "brag sheet" of your accomplishments to help them write better.
✏️ Start Essays
Draft your Common App and UC essays over the summer. Don't wait till 12th grade. Summer is your only free time — use it. Start with brainstorming.
🏫 College Visits
Visit colleges in person or virtually. Many offer free tours. Most UC and Cal State campuses are free to visit. Make a final list of 12–15 schools.
💰 Scholarships
Apply to QuestBridge (deadline Sept), Gates Scholarship, and local scholarships. Don't skip these — they can cover all 4 years of college.

📕 12th Grade — Execute. Apply. Win.

This is it. Every deadline matters. Stay organized and don't leave money on the table.

💸 FAFSA — Oct 1
File FAFSA the day it opens — October 1. Many schools give aid first-come first-served. Late filers miss out on thousands of dollars.
🏁 EA/ED — Nov 1
Early Action (EA) is non-binding and boosts your odds. Apply EA to your top school if they offer it. Don't miss this — it's your best shot.
🏫 UC — Nov 30
UC application deadline is November 30 for ALL campuses — UCLA, Berkeley, UCSD, etc. One application covers all 9 UC campuses. Don't miss it.
📝 RD — Jan 1
Most Regular Decision deadlines are January 1–15. Finish all essays BEFORE winter break — don't rely on the break to get it done.
📊 CSS Profile
Many private colleges require the CSS Profile for financial aid — separate from FAFSA. Submit it early. It can unlock institutional grant money.
🎉 May 1 — Commit!
National Decision Day is May 1. Compare financial aid offers — negotiate if needed. Commit to your school and celebrate. You did it!

🏫 UC Personal Insight Questions (PIQs) 2026

Choose 4 of 8 · 350 words each
📄
UC PIQ Brainstorm & Draft Doc
All 8 prompts in one Google Doc with brainstorm boxes, word count tracker, and tips for each. Click → File → Make a Copy → start writing.
📄 Open New Doc in Drive

Pick 4 prompts that each show a DIFFERENT side of you. Don't repeat themes. These are the official prompts used every year.

PIQ #1 — Leadership
Describe an example of your leadership experience in which you have positively influenced others, helped resolve disputes, or contributed to group efforts over time.
💡 Leadership doesn't need a title. Organizing your community, coaching a younger sibling, or stepping up in a crisis all count.
PIQ #2 — Creativity
Every person has a creative side. Describe how you express your creative side.
💡 Creativity = cooking, coding, fashion, fixing cars, problem-solving. It's not just art.
PIQ #3 — Talent
What would you say is your greatest talent or skill? How have you developed and demonstrated that talent over time?
💡 Be specific. Don't say "I'm good at math" — describe the exact moment that skill showed up in your life.
PIQ #4 — Educational Opportunity
Describe how you have taken advantage of a significant educational opportunity or worked to overcome an educational barrier you have faced.
💡 Mention internships from this site — USC YRP, Riordan, etc. Or: learning English, family responsibilities, changing schools.
PIQ #5 — Challenge
Describe the most significant challenge you have faced and the steps you have taken to overcome this challenge. How has this challenge affected your academic achievement?
💡 Authenticity wins. Show growth, not just hardship. How did you change because of this?
PIQ #6 — Academic Passion
Think about an academic subject that inspires you. Describe how you have furthered this interest inside and/or outside of the classroom.
💡 Connect it to real action — a project, a volunteer role, a program. Don't just say you love the subject.
PIQ #7 — Community
What have you done to make your school or your community a better place?
💡 Volunteer hours from this site (UCLA Health, LAPL, Cedars-Sinai) are perfect here. Use numbers: "served 200+ patients."
PIQ #8 ⭐ — Your Choice
Beyond what has already been shared in your application, what do you want us to know about you?
💡 Use this to fill a gap. Bilingual? Family caretaker? Unique job or tradition? If none of #1–7 fit, this is your prompt.

📝 Common App Personal Statement 2026

650 words max · Goes to ALL Common App schools
✍️
Common App Essay Brainstorm & Draft Doc
All 7 prompts in one Google Doc with brainstorm boxes, outline section, draft space, and a word count guide. Click → File → Make a Copy → start writing.
📄 Open New Doc in Drive

This essay goes to USC, LMU, and most private universities. Start with a specific scene — the reader should picture exactly where you are in sentence one.

Prompt 1Background & Identity
Some students have a background, identity, interest, or talent so meaningful their application would be incomplete without it. Share your story.
💡 Perfect for first-gen, immigrant, and bilingual students. Your story of navigating two worlds is powerful here.
Prompt 2Overcoming Obstacles
Recount a time when you faced a challenge, setback, or failure. How did it affect you, and what did you learn?
💡 Focus on growth and transformation — not just how hard things were. Show what you did with the experience.
Prompt 3Challenging a Belief
Reflect on a time when you questioned or challenged a belief or idea. What prompted your thinking? What was the outcome?
💡 Shows intellectual maturity. Write about a belief you changed through a real experience — at an internship, in class, or in your community.
Prompt 4Gratitude
Reflect on something that someone has done for you that has made you happy or thankful in a surprising way. How has this gratitude affected or motivated you?
💡 Underused — a genuine mentor, parent, or teacher story here can be incredibly moving.
Prompt 5Personal Growth
Discuss an accomplishment, event, or realization that sparked a period of personal growth and a new understanding of yourself or others.
💡 Most popular prompt. Make the growth feel earned through a specific story, not just "I became a better person."
Prompt 6What Captivates You
Describe a topic, idea, or concept you find so engaging it makes you lose all track of time. Why does it captivate you?
💡 Start with the specific moment you fell in love with the topic — not a generic origin story.
Prompt 7 ⭐Your Own Topic — Most Freedom
Share an essay on any topic of your choice.
💡 Best for students with a truly unique story — family tradition, unusual responsibility, or something that doesn't fit prompts 1–6.
Essay tip: Start with a specific scene. The reader should picture exactly where you are in the first sentence. Reveal something not shown anywhere else in your application. Read it out loud — if it doesn't sound like you, rewrite it.
